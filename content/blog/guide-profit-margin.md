# Profit Margin vs Markup: How to Calculate Both Correctly

Profit margin and markup are related but they are not the same percentage. Confusing them can lead to pricing mistakes, especially when setting product prices.

## Profit margin

Profit margin measures profit as a percentage of the selling price:

`Margin = (Selling Price − Cost) / Selling Price × 100`

If a product costs $60 and sells for $100, the profit is $40 and the margin is 40%.

Use the [Profit Margin Calculator](/tools/profit-margin-calculator) to check a price quickly.

## Markup

Markup measures profit relative to the cost:

`Markup = (Selling Price − Cost) / Cost × 100`

With the same $60 cost and $100 price, markup is 66.67%.

Both numbers are correct; they answer different questions.

## Why the distinction matters

A business may say “we use a 40% margin” while another says “we add a 40% markup.” Those pricing rules produce different selling prices.

When documenting pricing formulas, always name the denominator.

## Practical workflow

1. Record the true unit cost.
2. Decide whether the target is margin or markup.
3. Calculate the selling price.
4. Add taxes, fees, shipping, or payment costs where relevant.
5. Recalculate the actual margin after all costs.

## FAQ

### Is margin always lower than markup?

For a positive profit, margin is lower than markup because they use different denominators.

### Can I use markup to reach a target margin?

Yes, but the formula must be converted correctly. A target margin of 40% requires a markup of about 66.67%.

### Should VAT be included?

That depends on whether you are calculating business profit before or after tax. Keep the accounting definition consistent.
